# Dear-Data-100

1. Project Topic:
   A study of my phone usage during university lectures.

2. Research Question:
   How often do I check my phone during university lectures, and what patterns can I identify in my phone-checking habits?
   
3. Why I chose this topic?
   I chose this topic because checking my phone is something that happens in everyday life, including during university lectures. I am interested in finding out how often I actually check my phone and whether I do it more frequently than I realise.

Sometimes checking my phone can be an automatic habit, and I might not notice how often I do it. By collecting data over time, I will be able to reflect on my behaviour and identify patterns that I might not have noticed before.

I also chose this topic because it is personal and relatable. Many university students use their phones throughout the day, so the project could help me understand my own habits while exploring a wider issue related to attention and technology.

4. How will I collect my data?
   I will collect data during university lectures over several weeks until I have recorded at least 100 individual phone checks.
Each time I check my phone, I will record it as one observation. I will use a consistent definition of a phone check so that my data remains reliable.

For each observation, I aim to record:
Time: The approximate time during the lecture when I checked my phone.
Reason: Why I checked my phone, for example, to check a notification, reply to a message, look at social media, check the time or out of habit.
Lecture stage: Whether the check happened at the beginning, middle or end of the lecture.

I will record the observations in a spreadsheet so that I can organise the data before using it in Processing. I will only record information that is relevant to the project and will not collect other people's personal information.

 How will I represent my data?
      I plan to create a Processing sketch that represents each phone check visually. I am considering using a timeline in which each observation is displayed as a small circle or symbol.
The position of each symbol will represent when the phone check happened during the lecture, while different colours or shapes will represent the reason for checking the phone.
For example, one colour could represent checking messages, another could represent social media, and another could represent checking my phone out of habit.
My aim is to create a visual design that is more expressive than a standard bar chart while still allowing the viewer to understand the data.
